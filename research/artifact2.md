# Probing Attention Residuals: three experiments for a new interpretability surface

**Attention Residuals (AttnRes), released by MoonshotAI on March 16, 2026, replaces the fixed-weight residual stream in transformers with learned softmax attention over preceding layer outputs — and in doing so, exposes an entirely new class of interpretable objects that existing mechanistic interpretability tools can exploit.** Each layer l in AttnRes computes its input as h_l = Σ α_{i→l} · v_i, where α weights derive from a static per-layer pseudo-query vector w_l attending over RMSNorm-normalized earlier-layer outputs. This creates three novel targets for mechanistic analysis: the α weight distributions themselves (soft routing decisions over depth), the learned w_l vectors (per-layer routing preferences), and the block-level representations in the practical Block AttnRes variant. The mechanistic interpretability field has matured rapidly through 2025–2026, producing circuit tracing, cross-layer transcoders, conditional activation steering, and architecture-agnostic tooling that can be adapted — with specific modifications — to probe these new objects. Below, I synthesize the relevant landscape and propose three concrete, publishable experiments.

---

## The interpretability landscape as of early 2026

The past year has produced several developments directly relevant to analyzing AttnRes. **Anthropic's circuit tracing framework** (March 2025, open-sourced May 2025 at `safety-research/circuit-tracer`) introduced cross-layer transcoders (CLTs) that replace MLP layers with sparse, monosemantic features spanning multiple layers, enabling attribution graphs that trace information flow across depth. The key methodological innovation — **constrained patching** — freezes attention patterns and normalization denominators during interventions, preventing second-order effects that confound standard activation patching. This is especially important for AttnRes, where naive patching could alter α weights and cascade through the entire depth-routing structure.

On the SAE front, the field is shifting from per-layer SAEs toward **transcoders** (Dunefsky et al., NeurIPS 2025; Paulo et al., January 2025) and **crosscoders** (Lindsey et al., December 2024), which capture cross-layer features. **RouteSAE** (Shi et al., EMNLP 2025) integrates a lightweight routing mechanism with a shared SAE to extract features from multiple layers dynamically — a design philosophy closely aligned with AttnRes's depth-wise routing. Meanwhile, **TopK SAEs** have been shown to achieve significantly better feature consistency (PW-MCC ≈ 0.80) than standard architectures (Song et al., ICML 2025), addressing concerns raised by the "SAEs do not find canonical units" result at ICLR 2025.

For probing and lens methods, **LogitLens4LLMs** (Wang et al., February 2025) extends intermediate-layer prediction to Qwen-2.5 and Llama-3.1, while the **Backward Lens** (Katz et al., EMNLP 2024 Best Paper) projects gradients into vocabulary space. The **Curse of Depth** result (Sun et al., NeurIPS 2025) demonstrates that nearly half of deep layers in Pre-LN transformers contribute minimally due to exponential variance growth — precisely the problem AttnRes was designed to solve, and a phenomenon whose mitigation the α weights should make visible.

In the cross-layer architecture space, AttnRes sits within a lineage: **DenseFormer** (NeurIPS 2024) uses static learned scalar weights for depth-wise averaging, **Hyper-Connections** (ICLR 2025) splits the residual into n parallel streams with learned mixing matrices, **mHC** (DeepSeek, December 2025) constrains mixing to the Birkhoff polytope for stability, and **MUDDFormer** (ICML 2025) applies multiway dynamic dense connections framed explicitly as "depth-wise attention." AttnRes's unique contribution is using softmax attention with per-layer pseudo-queries — applying the attention mechanism itself along the depth axis.

The tooling ecosystem is ready. **NNsight v0.6** (ICLR 2025) wraps any PyTorch model with hook-based interventions, making it the most architecture-agnostic option for AttnRes. **TransformerLens v3.0** (beta, December 2025) introduces TransformerBridge, which can wrap HuggingFace models via custom adapters. **SAELens v6.37** works with any PyTorch model for SAE training. **Neuronpedia** (open-sourced March 2025) provides hosting and visualization infrastructure.

## Experiment 1: α-spectroscopy — exact per-source-layer logit decomposition

**Core idea.** In standard transformers, the logit lens projects cumulative residual stream states h_l into vocabulary space, yielding an approximation of each layer's "belief" about the next token. In AttnRes, the final layer's input is an *exact weighted sum* h_L = Σ α_{i→L} · v_i, where each v_i is a specific earlier layer's output. This enables what I call **α-spectroscopy**: projecting each weighted contribution α_{i→L} · v_i through the unembedding matrix W_U to obtain an *exact, model-computed* decomposition of the output logits into per-source-layer components. Unlike the standard logit lens (which is an approximation that degrades at early layers), this decomposition is mathematically exact because it exploits the linearity of the unembedding after the softmax-weighted sum.

**Method.** For each input token at position t and each source layer i:

1. Extract v_i^t (the output of layer i for token t) and α_{i→L}^t (the attention weight the final layer assigns to layer i) using NNsight hooks on the AttnRes model.
2. Compute the per-source logit contribution: logit_i^t = α_{i→L}^t · (W_U · LayerNorm(v_i^t)), applying the final layer norm and unembedding.
3. Aggregate into a **spectral decomposition matrix** S ∈ ℝ^{L × |V|} where S[i, w] is source layer i's contribution to word w's logit.

This extends to intermediate layers: for any target layer l, decompose h_l = Σ_{i<l} α_{i→l} · v_i into per-source components and project each through a **tuned lens** (Belrose et al., 2023, updated November 2025) trained for the AttnRes architecture. The tuned lens's per-layer affine translators would need to be retrained for AttnRes, since the intermediate representations are no longer cumulative sums but weighted mixtures.

**Contrastive analysis.** Compare α distributions and spectral decompositions across behavioral conditions: correct vs. incorrect predictions on GPQA-Diamond (where AttnRes shows **+7.5 points** over baseline), multi-hop reasoning vs. single-hop retrieval, and code generation vs. natural language. This draws on **Contrastive Activation Addition** methodology (Panickssery et al., 2023) but applied to the α weight space rather than the residual stream. Specifically, compute Δα = α_correct − α_incorrect averaged over paired examples, identifying which source layers are differentially recruited for successful reasoning.

**Tooling.** NNsight for activation extraction (architecture-agnostic, works immediately with AttnRes's PyTorch implementation). Custom visualization using CircuitsVis attention pattern components adapted for depth-wise attention heatmaps. Tuned lens retraining via the `tuned-lens` library with AttnRes-specific hooks.

**What makes this novel.** Standard logit lens is an *approximation* that works because residual stream representations iteratively refine toward the output; in AttnRes, the decomposition is *exact* because the model explicitly computes how much each layer contributes. The Curse of Depth predicts that standard Pre-LN transformers waste deep layers; α-spectroscopy would directly test whether AttnRes's depth-routing mechanism successfully redistributes computational load, providing the first empirical evidence at the mechanistic level for *why* AttnRes shows scaling law improvements. DenseFormer's analysis of static DWA weights (Pagliardini et al., NeurIPS 2024) showed stable cross-seed patterns; this experiment would reveal whether AttnRes's *input-dependent* routing (via content-aware keys) produces qualitatively richer patterns than DenseFormer's static ones.

**Grounding.** LogitLens4LLMs (Wang et al., 2025) for modern architecture support, Tuned Lens (Belrose et al., updated November 2025) for trainable layer decoders, Backward Lens (Katz et al., EMNLP 2024) for gradient-space extensions, Block Influence metric from ShortGPT (Men et al., ACL Findings 2025) as a comparison baseline for layer importance, and MoE Embedding (ICLR 2025) which showed routing decisions encode rich semantics.

## Experiment 2: routing-feature SAEs trained on α weight distributions

**Core idea.** The α weight vector for each layer l is an (l−1)-dimensional simplex point that encodes a soft routing decision over depth. Across all layers and tokens, these vectors form a high-dimensional dataset of routing decisions that may contain interpretable structure — "routing features" analogous to how MoE routing decisions have been shown to encode part-of-speech categories and topical specialization. Train **sparse autoencoders on the concatenated α weight distributions** across layers to discover latent routing patterns, then characterize these patterns semantically.

**Method.** For a given input sequence, AttnRes produces α_{i→l} for every target layer l and source layer i < l. In Block AttnRes with N = 8 blocks, this yields an **8 × 8 lower-triangular attention matrix** per token (plus attention to the token embedding). The experiment proceeds in three phases:

*Phase 1: Feature discovery.* Concatenate the α matrices across all layers into a single vector per token position (dimension ~36 for 8 blocks attending to up to 8 sources each). Collect these vectors over a large corpus (e.g., 1B tokens from a diverse dataset). Train TopK SAEs (using SAELens or Sparsify) on this routing space, with dictionary sizes ranging from 256 to 4096. TopK SAEs are chosen specifically because they provide **the best feature consistency guarantees** (PW-MCC ≈ 0.80, per Song et al., ICML 2025), ensuring discovered routing features are reproducible across training runs. This is critical for establishing that routing patterns are genuine model structure rather than SAE artifacts.

*Phase 2: Feature characterization.* For each discovered routing feature, identify its top-activating tokens and contexts using the Delphi automated interpretability pipeline (EleutherAI). Cross-reference with downstream task performance: do certain routing features activate preferentially during mathematical reasoning (where AttnRes shows largest gains), code generation, or factual recall? Use **feature sensitivity testing** (Tian et al., NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight) to verify that routing features activate reliably on semantically similar inputs, not just surface-level token patterns.

*Phase 3: Causal validation.* Apply **constrained patching** adapted from Anthropic's circuit tracing methodology. For a given routing feature f, identify the corresponding modification to α weights, intervene to suppress or amplify this routing pattern, and measure the effect on model outputs. The constrained patching approach is essential here: because α weights determine the computational graph itself, unconstrained interventions could produce out-of-distribution hidden states (per Grant et al., November 2025, who showed causal interventions often create divergent representations). Constrained patching prevents second-order cascading through the routing mechanism by freezing α weights at other layers during the intervention.

**Comparison with MoE routing interpretability.** This experiment directly parallels three recent MoE results. **Monet** (Park et al., ICLR 2025) showed that scaling to 262,144 experts produces monosemantic experts discoverable through routing patterns. **MoE-X** (Yang et al., ICML 2025) demonstrated that routing can be redesigned for intrinsic interpretability via sparsity-aware selection. **Expert specialization by POS** (Antoine et al., December 2024) showed linear probes achieve 80–89% POS accuracy from routing paths alone. AttnRes's α weights are structurally analogous to MoE routing: both are softmax-normalized selection weights over computational units. The key difference is that AttnRes routes over *depth* (which layer's representation to use) rather than *width* (which expert to activate), making discovered features interpretable as "depth-routing strategies" — a new category of mechanistic structure.

**Tooling.** NNsight for extracting α weight matrices from any AttnRes model. SAELens v6.37 or Sparsify for TopK SAE training (both framework-agnostic). Delphi for automated feature explanation. Neuronpedia for hosting feature dashboards and enabling community exploration. The **RouteSAE** approach (Shi et al., EMNLP 2025) provides a directly relevant architecture: its integration of routing with shared SAEs could be adapted to learn routing-aware features jointly with residual stream SAEs.

**What makes this novel.** No prior work has trained SAEs on soft routing distributions over depth. Existing SAE targets include residual stream activations, MLP outputs (transcoders), attention outputs (Kissane et al., ICML 2024 workshop), and attention patterns. The α weights are a fundamentally different object: they live on a simplex rather than in ℝ^d, they are low-dimensional (8–64 dimensions rather than thousands), and they directly encode the model's routing strategy. The low dimensionality means small SAE dictionaries (256–4096) may suffice, making training cheap and results more interpretable. This also connects to the **Archetypal SAE** work (Fel et al., ICML 2025), which constrains dictionary atoms to the data's convex hull — naturally appropriate for simplex-valued routing vectors.

## Experiment 3: w_l geometry and depth-routing circuits via adapted circuit tracing

**Core idea.** The pseudo-query vectors w_l ∈ ℝ^d are static learned parameters — one per layer — that determine each layer's default routing preferences over earlier layers. These vectors live in the same d-dimensional space as the model's hidden states and interact with earlier layer outputs through dot-product attention. This creates a natural question: **what do the w_l vectors encode, and how does their geometric structure relate to the model's computational organization?** Combine geometric analysis of w_l with an adapted version of Anthropic's circuit tracing to build "depth-routing circuits" — attribution graphs where edges pass through α weight computations.

**Method.** This experiment has three components:

*Component A: w_l geometry.* Analyze the learned pseudo-query vectors directly. Compute pairwise cosine similarities between all w_l vectors to identify clusters of layers with similar routing preferences. Project w_l vectors into the model's principal subspaces (computed via PCA on residual stream activations) to determine which representational dimensions the routing mechanism attends to. Compare the w_l similarity structure with the model's **Block Influence** (BI) redundancy patterns (Men et al., ACL Findings 2025) — layers with similar w_l vectors should show similar BI scores if the routing preferences track computational function. Apply **Representational Similarity Analysis** (RSA) between the w_l similarity matrix and task-performance matrices across benchmarks to identify whether routing geometry predicts task specialization.

*Component B: α-weighted attribution graphs.* Adapt Anthropic's circuit tracing methodology for AttnRes. The key modification: in standard transformers with CLTs, attribution flows through MLP features at each layer, with the residual stream providing fixed-weight skip connections. In AttnRes, the skip connections are replaced by α-weighted aggregation, meaning **every edge in the attribution graph must be weighted by the corresponding α value**. Concretely:

- Train cross-layer transcoders on the AttnRes model using the OpenMOSS Language-Model-SAEs framework (`github.com/OpenMOSS/Language-Model-SAEs`), which already supports CLT training with TopK and BatchTopK activations.
- Modify the attribution graph construction to account for α weighting: when computing the backward Jacobian for feature f at layer l, the contribution from source feature g at layer i must be scaled by α_{i→l} (the routing weight from layer i to layer l). This makes attribution edges *input-dependent in a structured way* — the graph topology is fixed (all-to-all connections exist) but edge weights vary with input through the α mechanism.
- Apply constrained patching with the additional constraint that α weights at non-target layers remain frozen, preventing routing decisions from cascading.

*Component C: steering via w_l perturbation.* The w_l vectors are d-dimensional parameters that control routing for an entire layer. Perturbing them is equivalent to globally redirecting which earlier layers a target layer attends to. Adapting **Conditional Activation Steering** (CAST, Lee et al., ICLR 2025 Spotlight), define "routing steering vectors" as differences Δw_l = w_l^{behavior_A} − w_l^{behavior_B}, computed by fine-tuning w_l on contrastive behavioral datasets while freezing all other parameters. Because w_l vectors are static parameters (not activations), this is closer to a targeted, interpretable weight edit than traditional activation steering. Apply **Feature Guided Activation Addition** (FGAA, ICLR 2025) in the α weight space: express routing steering vectors in terms of SAE features discovered in Experiment 2, enabling human-interpretable descriptions of how routing changes affect behavior.

**Tooling.** NNsight for all activation access and intervention. OpenMOSS Language-Model-SAEs for CLT training (supports distributed training, multiple SAE variants). Anthropic's circuit-tracer library (`safety-research/circuit-tracer`) for attribution graph construction and visualization, adapted with a custom `ReplacementModel` class that implements α-weighted aggregation. The attribution-graphs-frontend (`anthropics/attribution-graphs-frontend`) for interactive graph exploration. For w_l geometric analysis: standard scientific Python stack plus CircuitsVis for visualization.

**What makes this novel.** This is the first proposal to treat static routing parameters (w_l) as first-class interpretable objects amenable to geometric analysis, steering, and circuit integration. Anthropic's circuit tracing assumes fixed residual connections; adapting it to α-weighted connections produces attribution graphs where **routing decisions become visible as variable edge weights** rather than being hidden in the fixed computational graph. The w_l steering component exploits a unique property of AttnRes: because routing is controlled by a small set of d-dimensional vectors (one per layer, totaling ~Ld additional parameters), targeted edits to routing behavior are far more compact and interpretable than editing MLP weights or attention heads. This connects to the representation engineering taxonomy (Wehner et al., February 2025), extending it from activation-space steering to parameter-space steering on a naturally interpretable target.

## Feasibility and implementation roadmap

All three experiments assume access to a trained AttnRes model. MoonshotAI's repository (`github.com/MoonshotAI/Attention-Residuals`) provides the architecture code and training recipes; the Block AttnRes variant with N = 8 blocks on a 1–3B parameter model is trainable on a single 8×A100 node. For researchers without training compute, the scaling law experiments in the paper use models at **60M to 1B parameters** — small enough for academic budgets.

The recommended implementation order is **Experiment 1 → 2 → 3**, as each builds on the previous. Experiment 1 requires only activation extraction (achievable in a day with NNsight), producing immediate visualizable results. Experiment 2 requires SAE training on low-dimensional routing vectors (hours on a single GPU given the small dimensionality). Experiment 3 requires CLT training (the most compute-intensive step, requiring ~1 week on 8 GPUs based on Anthropic's reported training costs for Gemma-2-2B transcoders) and custom modifications to the circuit-tracer codebase.

- **Experiment 1** targets *NeurIPS 2026 Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop* — short paper, quick turnaround, strong visual results
- **Experiment 2** targets *ICML 2026* — full paper, connects to SAE evaluation literature and MoE routing interpretability
- **Experiment 3** targets *ICLR 2027* — ambitious full paper, extends Anthropic's circuit tracing to a new architecture class

## What these experiments would collectively reveal

Together, these three experiments address a fundamental question: **does making the residual stream input-dependent and learnable — rather than fixed — produce models that are more mechanistically interpretable, not just more capable?** AttnRes's +7.5 point gain on GPQA-Diamond suggests it improves multi-step reasoning; α-spectroscopy (Experiment 1) would show *which layers contribute to which reasoning steps*. Routing-feature SAEs (Experiment 2) would reveal whether the model develops *systematic routing strategies* for different cognitive tasks. And depth-routing circuits (Experiment 3) would determine whether α-weighted attribution graphs produce *cleaner, more interpretable circuits* than those found in standard transformers where residual connections hide the depth-wise information flow.

The DenseFormer analysis already showed that even static depth-weighting produces "surprisingly stable patterns" across seeds. AttnRes's content-aware routing should produce richer, input-dependent patterns. If these patterns are interpretable — if routing features correspond to meaningful cognitive strategies and w_l geometry tracks computational organization — it would establish a new paradigm: **architectures designed for efficiency that are simultaneously designed for interpretability**, where the routing mechanism serves as a built-in interpretability interface that existing tools can probe directly.
